<b>About me</b>

Hi, I'm <b>Alberto</b>, 23, and I am currently building my path in <b>Cybersecurity</b>, with a strong interest in working as a <b>Junior SOC Analyst</b>.  
I come from a <b>Full Stack web development</b> background, which helps me understand how systems and applications behave when I'm looking at logs, errors and events.

Right now, I'm focused on <b>learning the fundamentals properly</b>: understanding what I'm doing, not just clicking tools.



<b> SOC direction: learning to see the signal</b>

At the moment, I'm at an early but committed stage of my journey.  
I'm learning how a SOC works and how analysts turn raw data into security-relevant information.

<b>What I'm currently learning</b>

- <b>Monitoring & log analysis (foundations)</b>
  - Reading and interpreting basic logs from systems, web servers and applications
  - Understanding what "normal" looks like to spot what looks "suspicious"
  - Getting familiar with common attacker techniques at a high level (MITRE ATT&CK mindset)

- <b>Alert triage (junior level concepts)</b>
  - Basic idea of how alerts are generated and why false positives exist
  - How to group related events and start forming a simple timeline
  - Asking simple but important questions: *Is this expected? From where? For how long?*

- <b>Incident response basics (theory and labs)</b>
  - Introductory concepts of containment, eradication and recovery
  - How and when to escalate to a more senior analyst
  - The importance of documenting clearly what was observed and what was done

- <b>Process awareness</b>
  - Understanding that SOC work is not just tools, but <b>procedures, teamwork and communication</b>
  - Learning to think in terms of repeatable steps instead of "one-time tricks"



<b> Tools & technologies (learning stage)</b>

I'm progressively getting familiar with tools and technologies that are useful in or around a SOC.  
My goal is **not** to claim mastery, but to build a solid base and grow from there.

- <b>Operating systems & environments</b>: Linux basics, Kali Linux for labs
- <b>Network & traffic analysis</b>: Wireshark, Nmap
- <b>Web & application security</b>: Burp Suite, Browser dev tools
- <b>Scripting & automation</b>: Python and Bash for log parsing, text processing, automation
- <b>Version control & documentation</b>: Git and GitHub

I'm focusing on <b>understanding the concepts</b> behind each tool rather than just memorizing commands.



<b> Development background: my extra angle</b>

Before shifting my focus to cybersecurity, I studied and worked with **Full Stack web development**.  
This doesn't make me a senior developer, but it gives me a practical "under the hood" view that I can use as a junior analyst.

<b>Front-end</b>: HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript, TypeScript, Angular  
<b>Back-end</b>: PHP, Laravel  
<b>Databases & UI</b>: MySQL, Bootstrap  

This helps me to:
- Understand how an application is structured when I look at its logs or errors  
- See where security is often forgotten in real projects (validation, authentication, configuration)  
- Communicate more naturally with developers when discussing issues or improvements  



<b> How I like to learn and work </b>

- <b>Lab-focused learning</b> — Building small labs, reproducing scenarios and seeing what kind of logs and events are generated
- <b>Curious and honest about my level</b> — I'm junior, and I'm okay with that. What matters to me is <b>progress</b> and doing things with intention
- <b>Clear and structured communication</b> — Explaining what I see in a simple and structured way: <b>what happened, where, since when, and why I think it matters</b>

I'm actively looking for opportunities to learn in SOC-oriented environments (labs, projects, volunteering, internships), people and communities to share knowledge, and feedback that helps me become a better analyst, step by step.
